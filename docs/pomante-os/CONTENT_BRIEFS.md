## KC-001 — Çocuklar İçin İngilizce Kelime Oyunu Nasıl Seçilir?

### Brief

- **Audience and job:** A parent or caregiver of a child around primary-school end / middle-school start who wants to judge whether an English word game is suitable and useful without needing to be an English teacher.
- **Primary intent:** Learn how to choose an English word game for a child.
- **Supporting questions:** Is the game at the right level? What should the child actually do with the words? How much repetition and feedback is useful? How can a parent tell whether play is becoming practice? What privacy/access details should be checked?
- **Reader outcome:** Use a short checklist to accept, reject, or trial a game and run one simple follow-up check after play.
- **Content type:** Evergreen decision/use guide.
- **Canonical owner:** KC-001 owns the selection framework. It does not own free-game discovery, a list of games, or a “best games” comparison.
- **Product connection:** A short optional example may show how Poma Academy's verified football or volleyball experience uses lesson-progress questions. Link to LP-002 only after the framework; do not turn the guide into a product advert.
- **Primary user action:** Apply the checklist to one game. Secondary action: visit the English-game hub when it exists.
- **Measurement:** Organic impressions/clicks for selection/use questions; engaged guide view; checklist interaction if implemented; contextual hub click; assisted game start. No child identifiers or exact answers.
- **Owner/reviewer:** Content owner unassigned. Before publication, assign an editor and an English-learning/education reviewer for learning claims; privacy wording requires the appropriate qualified reviewer.
- **Review trigger:** Review six months after publication, or earlier if product mechanics, cited evidence, child/privacy guidance, internal destinations, or search intent changes.

### Required answer framework

Open with the answer: a suitable game matches the child's current knowledge and practice goal, asks the child to attend and respond rather than tap through without processing, gives understandable feedback, supports revisiting words, and is practical and safe for the family to use.

The body should help the reader assess:

1. **Goal:** Which small skill is being practised—recognising meaning, recalling a word, spelling, listening, or using it in a phrase?
2. **Level:** Can the child understand the task and succeed sometimes without the game becoming automatic or persistently frustrating? Use observed performance, not age alone.
3. **Action:** Does the child recall, match, say, hear, spell, or use English? Explain that these actions are different and should not be presented as interchangeable outcomes.
4. **Feedback:** Does the game make errors understandable and allow another attempt without shame or pressure?
5. **Return and transfer:** Can selected words reappear later, and can the child recognise or use one after the game in a different example?
6. **Practical fit:** Are instructions, session length, accessibility, ads, account requirements, cost, and privacy clear enough for the family?

Conclude with a compact trial routine: choose one goal, play a short round, ask the child to recognise or use two or three target words outside the game, note what was easy/difficult, then decide whether to repeat, lower/raise difficulty, or choose another activity. Do not prescribe a universal duration or promise an outcome.

### Proposed outline

1. Direct answer and six-point checklist.
2. First choose the practice goal.
3. Judge level by what the child can do, not a label alone.
4. Check the learning action, feedback, and meaningful repetition.
5. Run a simple after-game transfer check.
6. Review practical, accessibility, commercial, and privacy details.
7. Worked examples: one suitable-but-limited game, one poor fit, and one Poma Academy sports example clearly labelled as product evidence.
8. Printable/interactive checklist summary and the relevant next step.

### Evidence plan

- [British Council LearnEnglish Kids word games](https://learnenglishkids.britishcouncil.org/grammar-vocabulary/word-games) and [parent guidance](https://learnenglishkids.britishcouncil.org/parents/helping-your-child/how-start-teaching-kids-english-home) demonstrate distinct vocabulary practice activities and parent-supported use; they do not prove that any game guarantees learning.
- Gordon et al., [Fostering retention of word learning](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-child-language/article/fostering-retention-of-word-learning-the-number-of-training-sessions-children-retrieve-words-positively-relates-to-posttraining-retention/CAD724BCD77EB45BE82D698F6B832E6C) supports carefully qualified discussion of retrieval across sessions.
- Yu and Trainin, [technology-assisted L2 vocabulary learning meta-analysis](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/metaanalysis-examining-technologyassisted-l2-vocabulary-learning/08A549A6CFD1078406E6A4F8AFE28184) may contextualize digital vocabulary practice, but the draft must preserve its conditions and limitations rather than generalize to every child or game.
- Repository/product review supplies claims about Poma Academy mechanics. Product behavior is evidence only for what the product does, not for a learning-outcome claim.

The writer must read the full sources used, record access dates, cite the claim they actually support, and separate research evidence from editorial judgment. Add a current authoritative source for child privacy/digital-safety guidance only if the draft makes a specific claim in that area.

### Exclusions and quality gate

- No “best games” ranking, invented age bands, fixed daily-minutes prescription, or unsupported claim that fun/game mechanics cause learning.
- No competitor criticism, fabricated parent quotes, outcome guarantees, or claims based solely on vendor marketing.
- No generic filler history of gamification or long unstructured game list.
- Include limitations: a game is practice, not a complete language programme; recognition inside one interface may not equal independent recall or use.
- Use `Article` schema only when visible authorship, dates, headline, and full body satisfy the current eligibility rules. Do not add `FAQPage` merely because headings use questions.
- Draft does not enter production until the content owner and qualified reviewer are assigned.
